To Kill a Mockingbird

To Kill a Mockingbird

by Harper Lee

Classic / Social Fiction

Synopsis

A young girl observes racial injustice and moral courage in her Southern American community.

Chapter 1: A Hot Summer in Maycomb

Scout spent the summer climbing trees and daring her brother to touch the porch of the strange house at the end of the road. Everything felt simple, as summers do when you are eight.

Then her father took a case no one wanted. Neighbors stopped waving, and Scout learned that being brave sometimes means standing still while others stare.

Her father, Atticus, told her that you never truly understand a person until you climb into his skin and walk around in it. Scout frowned, because it sounded uncomfortable. But she kept the idea in her pocket for later.

That night, a lamp burned late in the study window, and she heard him reading aloud to no one. In the morning, the whole town knew which case he had taken. Scout held her brother's hand and walked to school with her chin up.

At school, a girl named Cecil sneered at Scout's father, and Scout's fists curled. Jem caught her sleeve. 'Count to ten,' he whispered, in the voice of someone who had just learned it himself. She counted to seven before she had to run to the swing set.

That evening, her father found her sulking on the porch. 'Courage,' he said, 'is deciding to be gentle when everyone expects you to be loud.' Scout frowned. 'Even if I'm right?' 'Especially then,' he said. The fireflies blinked in the dark, and for once Scout let the quiet win.

End of Chapter 1
