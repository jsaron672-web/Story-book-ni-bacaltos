A Brief History of Time

A Brief History of Time

by Stephen Hawking

Science / Nonfiction

Synopsis

Stephen Hawking introduces major ideas about the universe, including black holes, space, time, and the origins of the cosmos.

Chapter 1: Looking Up

Every night for as long as people have existed, someone has looked up and asked how it all began. For most of history, the answers were stories.

Then the questions became equations. A telescope here, a theory there, and the universe turned out to have a beginning, a shape and a stranger future than anyone had guessed.

The first astronomers drew circles, believing the sky was a perfect machine. Then a telescope found moons around Jupiter, and the machine cracked. The heavens turned out to be messier and more beautiful than anyone had dared to imagine.

Today we know the universe is expanding, that stars are born and die, and that even time bends near a black hole. Each answer opens a better question. And that, perhaps, is the most wonderful discovery of all.

Imagine a clock placed near a black hole. To someone far away, its ticks would stretch out, slower and slower, until the final second seemed to last forever. Time, it turns out, is not a river that flows the same everywhere. It bends, like a stretched sheet.

That is the strange lesson of modern physics: space and time are woven together, and gravity is the shape of that weave. We cannot see it with our eyes, but we can calculate it, test it and watch it come true. Looking up is the oldest science there is, and it has only just begun.

End of Chapter 1
