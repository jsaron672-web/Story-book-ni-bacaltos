The Martian

by Andy Weir

Science Fiction / Survival

Synopsis

An astronaut stranded on Mars uses scientific knowledge and ingenuity to survive while awaiting rescue.

Chapter 1: Log Entry One

I am alone on Mars, and my crew has left. They thought I was dead, which is fair. I would have thought so too, except I woke up and was not.

I have food for thirty days, air for ten, and a very large spreadsheet. Step one: do not panic. Step two: panic briefly, then do step one again.

The potato plan was my own idea. I had a few kilos left over from a Thanksgiving dinner we never got to have, a little water and a lot of human waste, which I will not describe in detail. Let's just say farming is not glamorous.

On Day Six, a green sprout poked out of the dirt. I cried. I am not ashamed. I named him Gerald, and I told him he was now the most important living thing on an entire planet.

By Day 12, I had fixed the radio. By fix, I mean I took apart three antennas and a coffee machine and taped the pieces together. It looks like a robot octopus. It may be the ugliest machine on two planets.

I switched it on. Static. More static. Then a tiny blip, a pattern, unmistakably not random. My hands shook. I tapped out a reply in the only code I could remember: 'I'm alive. Please send chocolate.' Then I sat down in the dust and laughed until I cried, which, I am told, is a normal human reaction.

End of Chapter 1
