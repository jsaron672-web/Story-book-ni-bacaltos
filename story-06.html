Pride and Prejudice

Pride and Prejudice

by Jane Austen

Romance / Classic

Synopsis

Elizabeth Bennet navigates love, family expectations, and social class while learning to question her first impressions.

Chapter 1: The Assembly Ball

Elizabeth had been told the ball would be dull, and the first hour proved it. Her mother whispered about fortunes, her sisters giggled, and a proud gentleman in the corner refused every dance.

When she overheard him call her 'tolerable,' she laughed rather than wept, and told the story twice before supper. She did not yet know that first impressions can be wrong in both directions.

The next afternoon, Mr. Bingley visited the neighboring house, and every Bennet girl lined up at the window to watch him ride away. 'He is very handsome,' Jane said, and blushed. Elizabeth said nothing, but she noticed that his friend never once smiled.

That evening, she wrote to her aunt, 'I have met a man of such pride that I think he must keep it in a safe.' She sealed the letter and went to bed, certain she would never think of him again.

A week later, Elizabeth wandered into a bookshop to escape her mother's matchmaking. The proud gentleman stood at the far shelf, holding the very novel she had wanted. 'You may have it,' he said stiffly. 'Thank you, but I prefer to wait,' she answered, 'until you finish pretending to read it.'

His mouth twitched, almost a smile, and Elizabeth marched out in a fury. All the way home, she composed brilliant insults. By the time she reached the gate, she realized she was still thinking about him. 'I shall have to dislike him more carefully,' she decided.
