Frankenstein

by Mary Shelley

Gothic Horror / Science Fiction

Synopsis

A scientist creates a living being, leading to tragic consequences and questions about responsibility and humanity.

Chapter 1: A Night of Lightning

Victor worked through the storm, sewing the final wire as thunder rattled the tower. The table sparked. Slowly, impossibly, a hand twitched against the sheet.

The eyes that opened were gentle and confused. Victor felt only horror and fled the room. Behind him, the creature sat up, alone for the first time, and wondered what he had done wrong.

The creature wandered into the forest and learned the world one painful lesson at a time. Fire was beautiful until it burned, and people were kind until they screamed. He hid in a barn and watched a family through the cracks in the wall.

From them, he learned language, laughter and the shape of a home. He longed to knock on their door. 'Surely,' he thought, 'they will see that I am gentle.' But the world is not always kind to those who look different.

He crept to the cottage door and knocked. An old blind man opened it and smiled at the sound of footsteps. 'Come in, friend,' he said, 'you must be cold.' For the first time in his life, the creature was welcomed. They sat by the fire and talked until the house was warm.

Then the family returned, saw him and screamed. A hand struck him, and another. He fled into the snow, clutching a bundle of papers he had found in his coat. They were a scientist's journals, full of details about his own creation, and one name, again and again: Victor. He learned that he had a maker, and that his maker had run.
