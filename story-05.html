Sherlock Holmes: The Hound of the Baskervilles

Sherlock Holmes: The Hound of the Baskervilles

by Arthur Conan Doyle

Mystery / Detective

Synopsis

Sherlock Holmes investigates a mysterious legend involving a terrifying hound and a wealthy family.

Chapter 1: The Walking Stick

The visitor left his walking stick behind, and Holmes had named the man's profession, pets and favorite pub before the kettle boiled. Watson tried not to look impressed and failed.

'Yet the stick is the smaller puzzle,' Holmes said. 'The howling on the moor, however, has kept three generations awake.' He reached for his coat. The game, as always, was afoot.

The next morning, Sir Henry Baskerville arrived, pale and shaken, with a letter that had been cut from newspaper words and pasted onto paper. 'Stay away from the moor,' it read. Holmes held it up to the light and smiled thinly.

'Someone wishes to keep you away, Sir Henry,' he said. 'Which means the moor holds something worth hiding.' Watson packed his pistol. If a ghostly hound truly lived out there, it was about to meet two very unreasonable men.

The night train to Devon rattled through the dark. Watson read the newspaper, Holmes read the passengers, and by dawn he knew which of them were lying about their luggage. At the hotel, Sir Henry stormed down in his socks. 'One of my boots has vanished,' he said. 'Why would anyone steal a single boot?'

Holmes tapped his chin. 'Because someone wishes to learn what the hound can smell.' Later, on the edge of the moor, a long, shaky howl drifted over the heather. 'A real hound,' Holmes murmured, 'does not howl in tune with the wind.'

End of Chapter 1
