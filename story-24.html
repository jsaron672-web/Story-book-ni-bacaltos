The Giver

The Giver

by Lois Lowry

Dystopian / Young Adult

Synopsis

A boy chosen to inherit his community's memories discovers the hidden costs of living in a seemingly perfect society.

Chapter 1: Assignment Day

Everyone in the community received a job at twelve, and everyone clapped politely. When Jonas's number was skipped, the clapping stopped.

The Chief Elder said only, 'Jonas has been selected.' She did not say for what, and Jonas felt the quiet pressing on his shoulders like a snowfall he could not remember.

The Giver was an old man in a quiet room. He placed his hands on Jonas's back and, for a moment, something rushed in: sunshine, color, the warm weight of snow. Jonas gasped. He had never seen red before.

'It will hurt,' the old man said, 'because memory is not only light.' Jonas looked at the plain gray walls of the community, and for the first time he wondered who had decided to take the colors away.

The old man showed him a memory of a sled gliding down a snowy hill, and Jonas laughed out loud for the first time in his life. Then the old man showed him a darker memory, of loud noise and falling soldiers. Jonas wept and could not stop.

That evening, his family spoke of schedules and neat bedtimes, and Jonas could not explain anything. He looked at his little sister, sleeping in her comfort, and understood at last that sameness was not safety. It was silence. And somewhere beneath the quiet, the world was still full of color.

End of Chapter 1
