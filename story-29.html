The Diary of a Young Girl

The Diary of a Young Girl

by Anne Frank

Biography / Historical Nonfiction

Synopsis

Anne Frank's diary documents her thoughts, experiences, and hopes while hiding with her family during World War II.

Chapter 1: Dear Kitty

The notebook arrived on her birthday, wrapped in checkered cloth. A year later the family vanished behind a bookcase into rooms no one outside knew existed.

She named the notebook 'Kitty' and told it everything: the whispers, the shared meals, the hope. 'Paper is more patient than people,' she wrote, and began again each morning.

In the Secret Annex, the days moved like slow clocks. The adults whispered, the radio murmured and the sun was a thing seen through a curtain. Anne wrote about it all, the quarrels, the jokes, the longing for open sky.

She wrote about the girl she was and the woman she hoped to become. 'I want to go on living even after my death,' she confided. Every morning, she dipped her pen again, determined that her voice would not be shut inside a wall.

Some nights the sirens sounded, and the Annex held its breath. Anne counted the pigeons on the roof, memorized the creaks of the stairs and sketched the single chestnut tree she could see through the attic window. Its leaves were the only clock she trusted.

One evening she wrote, 'Today I was angry, then funny, then brave, in that order.' It made her smile. Outside, the world was still dangerous, but inside the little notebook, she was free. She blew on the ink, closed the cover and slept.

End of Chapter 1
