Murder on the Orient Express

Murder on the Orient Express

by Agatha Christie

Mystery / Crime

Synopsis

Detective Hercule Poirot investigates a murder aboard a luxury train trapped by snow.

Chapter 1: The Train Stops

At midnight the train shuddered and stopped in a snowdrift. At dawn a passenger was found dead in his locked compartment, and the door chain was still fastened from the inside.

Poirot smoothed his mustache. 'Twelve suspects, one train and no way out,' he said happily. 'Someone here is lying beautifully. Let us find out who.'

Poirot interviewed each passenger in the dining car: the elegant countess, the nervous secretary, the retired colonel with a limp. Each had an alibi, and each alibi was almost perfect. 'Almost,' Poirot said, 'is the most beautiful word in my profession.'

He studied a burned note in a washbasin, a woman's handkerchief with a strange initial and a broken watch frozen at 1:15. The pieces did not match. Which meant someone, somewhere on this train, had arranged them that way on purpose.

Poirot questioned the conductor, who swore that no one had left the train. He examined the snow outside and found no footprints. He examined the passengers and found a strange habit among them: each mentioned the smell of lavender, though the dead man had been horribly allergic to it.

'Someone wishes us to believe the victim was alone,' Poirot said, smoothing his mustache. 'But a lie told by one person is a lie. A lie told by twelve is a rehearsal.' He looked down the dining car at the faces watching him. 'Mesdames and messieurs, shall we begin the performance?'

End of Chapter 1
